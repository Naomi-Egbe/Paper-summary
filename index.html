Paper summary

Paper: Liu, D., Wang, Z., Peng, C., Wang, N., Hu, R., & Gao, X. “Thinking Racial Bias in Fair Forgery Detection: Models, Datasets and Evaluations.” arXiv preprint, 2024.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14367

Problem

Most deepfake detection systems rarely examine datasets that adequately represent African faces. African faces constitute only about 3.8% of the FaceForensics++ dataset. This results in uneven detection performance across underrepresented groups.

Approach

To address this limitation, the researchers developed a dataset called FairFD. The dataset is described as balanced because it represents Caucasian, Asian, African, and Indian subjects. FairFD contains about three thousand subjects per demographic subgroup. The researchers also designed new fairness metrics and a pruning method called BPFA.

Evidence

The researchers tested 12 detectors trained on the FaceForensics++ dataset and found high racial bias. The least fair detector had a 17.65% gap, while the fairest had a 2.03% gap.

Lens

I looked at this paper through an ethics and society lens. A detector that works well overall can still fail certain groups, like African faces, and that is unfair to the very people it is meant to protect.
